Almost no host knows what a booking costs them. They know the percentage on
the screen, which is not the same thing.

Let us do the whole sum on an ordinary house: €110 a night, four-night
stays, eight nights booked a month.

## What you see and what you don't

Under the model Airbnb applies to most individual hosts in Europe, the host
commission sits around 15.5% and carries VAT on top. That is the visible
part.

The part you don't see: the guest pays their own service fee too. It doesn't
come out of your pocket, but it does come out of the total price they
compare against other options. Your house looks dearer than what you charge.

## The sum, month by month

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Two four-night stays at €110 | €880 |
| Host commission, 15.5% | −€136.40 |
| VAT on that commission, 21% | −€28.64 |
| **You keep** | **€714.96** |

That is **€165 a month** that never arrives. Close to **€1,980** a year.

## The same booking, direct

If that same guest books on your own page, the money lands in your Stripe
account. Stripe charges for processing the payment, which is a real service
and not a fee for introducing anyone: in Spain it runs around 1.5% plus
€0.25 per transaction for European cards, and in Mexico about 3.6% plus 3
pesos.

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| The same two stays | €880 |
| Processing, ~1.5% + €0.25 × 2 | −€13.70 |
| Booking Page, 79 USD a month | ≈ −€73 |
| **You keep** | **€793.30** |

Difference: **€78 more a month** on a house billing €880. And from the
second house on, the fee doesn't rise per booking: the margin grows on its
own.

## Where this calculation is unfair

It isn't free and I don't want to sell it that way.

**The platform brings you people who had never heard of you.** That is worth
paying for. If every booking you get comes from searches inside Airbnb,
removing the platform doesn't leave you better off, it leaves you empty.

**The fee is fixed and bookings are not.** In a slow month the 79 USD is due
all the same. At two stays a month it pays for itself; at one, barely.

**Moving people to direct booking is work.** You have to ask the guest who
already stayed, put the link on your Instagram, send it on checkout. Nobody
arrives on their own.

## So when does it make sense

When you already have guests who come back, referrals, or people who find
you by name. Those bookings were never brought in by the platform: they were
paying a commission on work you did yourself.

The rest can keep arriving the way it already does.

## Check the sum with your own numbers

Rates vary by country and by host type. On
[the Booking Page site](/booking-page/en/) there is a calculator where you
put in your rate, your nights and your currency, and it runs this same sum
on your figures.
