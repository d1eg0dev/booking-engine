It is November and Easter week at your house in Mexico is already booked. A family of
five, four nights, the whole house. In March they write: they can't make it after all.

What happens next depends on a rule you picked months ago. Often without thinking about
it much.

## What Airbnb lets you choose

Airbnb has four standard cancellation policies for stays under 28 nights. This is how
its Help Center describes them:

| Policy | Full refund if they cancel | After that |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flexible | Up to 24 hours before check-in | You are paid the nights stayed plus one more |
| Moderate | Up to 5 days before | You are paid one night plus 50% of the unused nights |
| Limited | Up to 14 days before | Between 7 and 14 days, 50% refund. Under 7 days, you are paid in full |
| Firm | Up to 30 days before | Between 7 and 30 days, 50% refund. Under 7 days, you are paid in full |

All four also have a 24-hour window. If the guest booked at least 7 days ahead, they can
cancel within 24 hours and get everything back. The strictest policies are available
only by invitation from Airbnb.

It is a well-designed menu. It protects the guest, who books with more confidence, and it
gives you clear options. For someone staying with you for the first time, it works.

## What it feels like to pick from a menu

Now think about your Easter week under Firm, the most protective of the four. If the
family cancels 31 days out, you refund everything. Those nights go back on your calendar
and you have to fill them again.

It is not a bad rule. It is a rule you did not write. It works the same for a city
apartment as for a villa on the coast in high season. And your house at Easter is not
just any date.

## Your page, your rule

When the booking is yours, so is the policy. You write it, in Spanish and English, and it
appears on your page for anyone who wants to book.

It can be as simple as this: full refund up to 60 days before arrival, 50% between 30 and
60 days, no refund under 30. It can be one rule for Easter and New Year and another for
the rest of the year. Or you can offer to move the dates instead of refunding.

What changes is not just the deadline. The guest who comes back every year knows you, and
the rule is yours. If something happens to them one year, you sort it out between you.

## Worth knowing before you start

**A clear rule builds trust.** Write it with the returning guest in mind. If it is much
stricter than the platforms', a first-time guest may prefer to book on Airbnb, and that
is fine: that is what Airbnb is for.

**If a refund is due, you make it.** It takes a few minutes from your Stripe account.
That is why it pays to write the rule with no room for doubt: it saves awkward
conversations.

**Keep it within the law.** In Mexico, guests are protected as consumers too. If you are
unsure how to word it, one session with a lawyer sets it up for years.

**Airbnb is still your front door.** New guests find you there. Your page is so the ones
who come back can book with you, on your rules.

## Where to start

Write down today, in two lines, how you want a cancellation to work for your Easter week.
Then compare it with the policy you have on Airbnb. If they don't match, you know what
rule you would put on your own page.
