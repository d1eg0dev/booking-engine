What a platform booking really costs

November 13, 2026 · Updated November 21, 2026 · Diego Larios · 2 min read

The Airbnb host fee you see is not the one you pay. The full arithmetic on published rates, for a house at €110 a night.

What this article is built on: Airbnb and Stripe published rates as of November 2026, with the arithmetic worked through

Almost no host knows what a booking costs them. They know the percentage on the screen, which is not the same thing.

Let us do the whole sum on an ordinary house: €110 a night, four-night stays, eight nights booked a month.

What you see and what you don't

Under the model Airbnb applies to most individual hosts in Europe, the host commission sits around 15.5% and carries VAT on top. That is the visible part.

The part you don't see: the guest pays their own service fee too. It doesn't come out of your pocket, but it does come out of the total price they compare against other options. Your house looks dearer than what you charge.

The sum, month by month

ItemAmount
Two four-night stays at €110€880
Host commission, 15.5%−€136.40
VAT on that commission, 21%−€28.64
You keep€714.96

That is €165 a month that never arrives. Close to €1,980 a year.

The same booking, direct

If that same guest books on your own page, the money lands in your Stripe account. Stripe charges for processing the payment, which is a real service and not a fee for introducing anyone: in Spain it runs around 1.5% plus €0.25 per transaction for European cards, and in Mexico about 3.6% plus 3 pesos.

Difference: €78 more a month on a house billing €880. And from the second house on, the fee doesn't rise per booking: the margin grows on its own.

Worth knowing before you start

The platform brings people who did not know you, and that is worth something. Your own page does not replace it: it complements it, for the guests who are already yours.

The fee is fixed. With two direct stays a month it pays off; with one, barely. Start when you already have guests who come back.

Returning guests need an invitation. Ask the guest who already stayed, put the link on your Instagram, and send it when they leave.

So when does it make sense

When you already have guests who come back, referrals, or people who find you by name. Those bookings were never brought in by the platform: they were paying a commission on work you did yourself.

The rest can keep arriving the way it already does.

Check the sum with your own numbers
