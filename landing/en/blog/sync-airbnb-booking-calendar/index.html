Airbnb, Booking and your own page at once, without the fear of a double booking

November 24, 2026 · Diego Larios · 3 min read

How to sync your Airbnb calendar with Booking and your own page, and what to do in the hours it takes to update.

What this article is built on: Airbnb Help Center (article 99): the calendar updates automatically every 3 hours and can be refreshed by hand; checked on 24 November 2026

A direct booking comes in for New Year at your place in Mexico. Five nights, a family who already knows the house. You are glad, and ten minutes later the doubt kicks in: what if someone books it on Airbnb too?

That doubt is what stops a lot of hosts from having their own page. It has a fix, and the fix takes five minutes.

How your calendars talk to each other

Airbnb, Booking and your page pass booked dates to each other through a calendar link called iCal. Each one exports its own and reads the others. Airbnb recommends it when you list in several places, precisely so two guests don't book the same dates.

What travels is the booked dates, not prices or guest details. And it does not travel instantly: according to its Help Center, Airbnb's calendar updates on its own every 3 hours. It also has a button to refresh it on the spot.

That is the whole secret. The hours between one update and the next are the only window where a double booking can slip in.

What it feels like to have three calendars

At first it looks like more work: three places a booking can come in. In practice it is the opposite. Once all three are connected, you stop checking each one with that knot in your stomach.

Your page stops being one more risk and becomes what it is: the place where the guests who know you book, on your rules, while Airbnb and Booking keep bringing in new ones.

A five-minute routine

Connect both ways. Each calendar has to export its own and import the others. If it only goes one way, half your bookings block nothing.

Name every imported calendar. "Booking, beach house" makes sense in March. A link with no name doesn't.

When a direct booking comes in, refresh by hand. Open your Airbnb calendar and use the refresh button. Check Booking too. Two minutes, and the window is closed.

In high season, block as soon as it's confirmed. For Easter, Christmas and New Year, block the dates by hand on the platforms as soon as you have the booking. That is when the most people book at once.

Run a test today. Block one weeknight on your page and see how long it takes to show up on Airbnb and Booking. Then you know how wide your window really is.

Worth knowing before you start

iCal is not real time, with anyone. Booking Page also syncs through iCal, so the window exists there too. The routine above is what closes it.

With many properties, there are faster tools. If you manage several houses with high turnover, a channel manager syncs more often and costs more. For one house or a couple, iCal and a good routine are usually enough.

Airbnb and Booking are still your shop window. Connecting the calendars is exactly what lets you be everywhere at once without losing control.
