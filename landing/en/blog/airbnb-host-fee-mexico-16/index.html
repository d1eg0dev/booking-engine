Airbnb's 16% in Mexico, explained calmly

November 30, 2026 · Diego Larios · 3 min read

The Airbnb host fee in Mexico is 16%. What changed, why guests barely notice, and what you can decide.

What this article is built on: Airbnb Help Center (article 1857): split fee (4% for the host in Mexico and 14.1 to 16.5% for the guest) and single 16% host fee in Mexico, VAT included where applicable; checked on 24 November 2026

You open your payout summary and something doesn't add up. Same house in Mexico, same nightly rate, and less money than last year.

You did nothing wrong. The way Airbnb charges changed, and it is worth understanding calmly, because it can be fixed.

What changed

Airbnb has two ways of charging its fee. This is how its Help Center explains them for Mexico:

Split feeSingle fee
Host pays4%16%
Guest pays14.1 to 16.5% of the subtotalNothing on top

With the split fee, guests saw a service fee on top of your price. With the single fee, it all comes from your side and the guest sees one price. Airbnb says the split fee is being phased out and that the single fee is already mandatory for most hosts. The fee you see includes VAT where applicable.

The numbers, without a scare

A house at 2,500 pesos a night, before taxes:

Per nightBefore, splitNow, unchangedNow, adjusted price
Your price2,5002,5002,857
What the guest pays2,853 to 2,9132,5002,857
What you keep2,4002,1002,400

If you change nothing, you get 300 pesos less per night. Raise your price to 2,857 and you keep 2,400 again. And the guest pays about what they paid before, because the fee they used to see separately is now inside the price.

What it feels like

It is normal to be annoyed at a smaller payout. What changed is a rule you did not write, and nobody likes that.

But this is not a reason to worry, it is a reason to decide. Airbnb still does its part well: putting you in front of travellers who don't know you. What is in your hands is the price, and where the guests who already know you book.

What you do decide

Your price on Airbnb. To keep the same amount at 16%, divide what you want to receive by 0.84. For 2,400 pesos, that is 2,857.

Where your returning guests book. On your own page there is no platform fee. You pay what Stripe charges for the card and the Booking Page fee. We ran that full sum with Mexican rates here.

Your rules. Seasons, minimum stay, cancellations. On your page, you write them.

Worth knowing before you start

Compare before you raise. Check how your total price looks next to similar homes in your area on Airbnb. A reasonable adjustment barely shows; a sharp one does.

VAT, with your accountant. Airbnb's fee includes VAT "where applicable". If you invoice, your accountant will tell you how to credit it.

Your own page is for the guests who already know you. Booking Page has a fixed fee and pays for itself once you have guests who come back.

Airbnb is still your shop window. New guests find you there, and that is worth something.

Where to start

Open your last Airbnb payout and check which fee you were charged. Then run the sum with your price: what you want to receive, divided by 0.84. In five minutes you know whether to adjust.
